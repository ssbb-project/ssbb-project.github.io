# How the NDO–AMP Library Works

The **NDO–AMP Library** is an open-access resource developed within the  
**Stop Spread Bad Bugs (SSBB)** MSCA consortium. It provides curated structural and experimental data for:

- **Non-digestible oligosaccharides (NDOs)**
- **Antimicrobial peptides (AMPs)**

The goal is to support research related to antimicrobial resistance, microbiome modulation, and carbohydrate–peptide interactions by making high-quality, well-documented data accessible to the scientific community.

---

## Data Collection Workflow

The NDO–AMP Library is built through a standardized multi-step workflow.

### 1. Sample Generation and Acquisition

- NDOs are synthesized, isolated, or supplied by consortium partners.  
- AMPs are designed, synthesized, or obtained from participating laboratories.  
- Each sample is assigned a unique identifier and linked to its origin (partner, project, or study).

### 2. Structural Characterization

For each NDO and AMP, structural information may include:

- **NMR data (¹H, ¹³C, etc.)**  
- **Mass spectrometry (MS) profiles**  
- **Chromatographic data (HPLC, LC–MS, etc.)**  
- **Predicted or experimentally determined 3D structures**

Whenever possible, structures are stored in standardized formats such as:

- **SMILES** and **InChI** for small molecules and carbohydrates  
- **FASTA** for peptide sequences

### 3. Functional and Microbiological Testing

Experimental data may cover:

- Antimicrobial activity against selected strains  
- Prebiotic or microbiome-modulating effects  
- Biofilm inhibition or disruption assays  
- Other relevant microbiological or biochemical readouts

All experiments are accompanied by basic metadata (e.g. conditions, concentration, assay type).

### 4. Curation and Quality Control

Before data are added to the public library:

- Entries are checked for consistency and completeness.  
- Structural and experimental data are reviewed by domain experts within SSBB.  
- When applicable, data are cross-referenced with external databases or literature.  

Only curated and approved entries are exposed through the NDO–AMP Library interface.
